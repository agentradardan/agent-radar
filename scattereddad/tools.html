Can't see the day

Time blindness is real. These planners put the day in front of you so you can see what actually fits.

My takeWhere I'd start: a free visual planner (Tiimo or Structured) before paying for an auto-scheduler.

Sunsama

No free plan

Daily planner that pulls tasks from your other work tools into one plan for the day, which you then time-block on your calendar.

Why it might help: A guided daily planning step asks you to pick what fits in today, which can make an overloaded list easier to cut down.

Price: Pro $17/month billed yearly or $22/month billed monthly. Enterprise: contact sales. Free trial with no credit card required; Sunsama says it has no free-forever plan. Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Motion

No free plan

AI calendar and project manager that places your tasks into open time on your calendar and reshuffles them when plans change.

Why it might help: Decides when tasks happen for you, so you spend less time building a schedule by hand.

Price: Pricing page lists Pro AI at $19/month and Business AI at $29/month per seat, with annual billing shown as saving 33%. No free plan listed. Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Reclaim.ai

Free plan

AI calendar assistant for Google and Outlook Calendar that automatically protects focus time, habits and breaks around your meetings.

Why it might help: Puts recurring habits (like lunch or a daily review) on your calendar and moves them when meetings land.

Price: Lite free for one user. Starter $10/seat/month and Business $15/seat/month billed yearly ($12 and $18 billed monthly). Enterprise $22/seat/month, yearly only. Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Tiimo

Free plan

Visual daily planner designed for neurodivergent people, with a timeline of your day, a focus timer, to-dos and AI help splitting tasks into subtasks.

Why it might help: Shows the day as colored blocks on a timeline, so you can see how much time is left before the next thing.

Price: Free version on iOS and Android (visual planner, focus timer, limited AI features). Tiimo Pro is monthly or yearly; Tiimo says the price varies by location and is shown at checkout. 7-day trial on the yearly plan. Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Can't get started

When a task feels like a wall, these break it into a first step small enough to actually take.

My takeWhere I'd start: Goblin Tools. Free website, no account, no excuses.

Goblin Tools

Free plan

Collection of small single-task AI tools made mostly for neurodivergent people. Magic ToDo breaks a task into smaller steps; other tools help with tone, time estimates and more.

Why it might help: Paste in a task that feels too big and get a list of smaller first steps, with no account needed on the website.

Price: Website tools are free with no ads or paywalls. Mobile apps cost a small one-time price. Optional Goblin Tools Pro subscription for some advanced features (price shown at sign-up). Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Can't stay on it

Focus sessions with a real person or the right sound, so the next 25 minutes go to one thing.

My takeWhere I'd start: Focusmate's free plan (3 sessions a week) to see if working next to someone helps.

Focusmate

Free plan

Virtual coworking: you book a short video session with another person, each say your goal, and work side by side (often called body doubling).

Why it might help: A booked session with a real person gives you a set start time and someone working alongside you.

Price: Free: 3 sessions per week, no credit card. Plus $8/month billed annually or $12/month billed monthly for unlimited sessions. Business plans available. Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Head's too full

Get it out of your head and into one place, fast, before it evaporates.

My takeWhere I'd start: whatever's already on your phone. Voice capture beats typing.

Akiflow

No free plan

Task and calendar app that collects tasks from email and other apps into one inbox, with quick capture, time blocking and a daily planning routine.

Why it might help: Turns emails and scattered to-dos into one list you can drag onto your calendar.

Price: Pro $34/month billed monthly or $19/month billed yearly ($228/year). 7-day trial requires a card. No free plan. Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Saner.AI

Free plan

AI assistant that brings notes, tasks, email and calendar into one place and suggests what to work on next.

Why it might help: Lets you dump notes and emails in one inbox and asks the AI what needs attention, instead of checking several apps.

Price: Free plan (30 AI messages/month, 100 notes). Starter $8/month; Standard $16/month with unlimited AI messages under a fair-use policy. Pricing page shows 20% off for yearly billing. Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Todoist

Free plan

To-do list app with natural-language Quick Add, reminders, and Ramble, a voice feature that turns what you say into separate tasks with dates.

Why it might help: Ramble lets you say several things at once and get them back as separate tasks, which suits quick brain dumps.

Price: Beginner free (5 personal projects, limited Ramble sessions). Pro $5/month billed yearly ($60) or $7/month billed monthly. Business $8/user/month yearly or $10 monthly. Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).

Workspace for notes, documents and databases, with Notion AI and Notion Agent for chat, drafting and autofill.

ChatGPT

Free plan

OpenAI's AI assistant, with voice chat on web and mobile that you can use to talk through a brain dump and get it back as a list.

Why it might help: Talk out everything on your mind, then ask it to sort the mess into a short list with a first step for each item.

Price: Free plan available. Paid Go, Plus and Pro plans for individuals (prices shown at checkout). Scheduled tasks are not included on the Free plan. Checked November 9, 2026 on the official pricing page (opens in a new tab).
