How a Grok Bot assistant can help with ADHD

Summary

Grok Bot is an AI assistant that can chat, take voice notes, connect to apps such as Gmail and Google Calendar, and run repeated tasks as scheduled routines. This page gives 5 copy-ready setup recipes for people with ADHD: Morning routine reminders, Brain dump to task list, Weekly review, Appointment prep, Body-doubling style check-ins. Each recipe keeps the user in control: the Bot drafts and reminds, and changes to calendars or emails wait for approval. Not medical advice: Grok Bot is not a clinician and does not diagnose or treat ADHD.

A lot of ADHD friction is remembering: the routine, the appointment, the thing you said you'd do. An AI assistant that can send you messages on a schedule can carry some of that load. Here are five setups for Grok Bot that you can copy and adjust.

What Grok Bot can do (from its docs)

  • Chat by text, voice note or voice call, on desktop, mobile, Slack or X.
  • Connect apps you already use, such as Gmail, Google Calendar, Notion, Slack and Google Drive.
  • Turn a task you ask for repeatedly into a routine that runs on a schedule you set.
  • Remember your preferences, so you don't have to re-explain your setup.
  • Draft emails and messages and leave them for you to approve and send.

Source: Grok Bot overview (official docs), checked November 9, 2026. Features and plans can change; check the docs for current details.

Five setup recipes

Replace anything in [BRACKETS] with your own details, then send it to your Bot. For the scheduled ones, ask it to make the task a routine and check the saved schedule.

1. Morning routine reminders

A short checklist at the same time every weekday, so the routine is not something you have to remember.

Every weekday at 7:00 AM, send me my morning checklist: [TAKE KEYS, WALLET, PHONE CHARGER; KIDS' BACKPACKS; ANYTHING ELSE]. Then list today's calendar events with the time I need to leave for each. End by asking me which one thing matters most today. Keep it under 10 lines.

Grok Bot turns a repeated request into a routine that runs on the schedule you set. Check the routine's schedule and your time zone after you create it.

2. Brain dump to task list

Say or type everything on your mind in one messy message, and get back a short, sorted list.

Here is my brain dump: [EVERYTHING ON YOUR MIND, IN ANY ORDER]. Sort it into Today, This week, Later and Not mine. For each Today item, give me one tiny first step that takes under 5 minutes. Don't add anything I didn't say. Keep it short.

You can send a voice note instead of typing. If you use a to-do app such as Todoist or Notion and have connected it, you can ask the Bot to add the Today items there.

3. Weekly review

A Sunday look at the week ahead, so Monday doesn't arrive as a surprise.

Every Sunday at 6:00 PM, read my Google Calendar for the coming week. Flag clashes, packed days and early starts. List anything I told you I would do this week. Then send me a short plan for Monday with no more than 3 priorities. Don't create, move or cancel any event without asking me.

This needs Google Calendar connected. Grok Bot's own docs describe a very similar Sunday-evening calendar routine.

4. Appointment prep

The evening before, a list of what's coming tomorrow and what to bring, so nothing gets missed at the door.

Every evening at 8:00 PM, look at tomorrow's calendar. For each appointment, tell me the time, when I need to leave, what to bring (paperwork, ID, payment) and any questions I wrote down for it. If something has no address or details, flag it. Don't change any event.

Keep sensitive details out of the reminder text if other people can see your phone. You decide what you share with the Bot.

5. Body-doubling style check-ins

Short check-in messages during a work block, to help you notice when you have drifted.

Today from 1:00 PM to 3:00 PM, check in with me every 30 minutes. Ask in one line what I'm working on and whether I'm still on it. If I say I drifted, ask me for the next tiny step and nothing else. Stop at 3:00 PM.

These are messages from an AI, not a live person. If you want a real person working alongside you, see Focusmate in our tool list.

Good habits for any AI assistant

  • Start with one recipe. Add another only after the first one sticks for a week or two.
  • Keep "ask me before sending, booking or changing anything" in your instructions.
  • Double-check important dates and times. AI assistants can make mistakes.
  • Think about what you share. Avoid pasting health records, passwords or financial account numbers into any chat.
  • An assistant can remind and organize. It isn't a doctor, coach or therapist.

In our view: reminders and brain-dump sorting are the easiest wins to start with, because they take two minutes to set up and need no new app.
