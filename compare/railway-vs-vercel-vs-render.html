Railway vs Vercel vs Render

Updated November 2, 2026. Prices and limits are as of Nov 2026, taken from each vendor's official pricing page (linked below). Check the vendor's site for current pricing.

Summary

All three deploy your code from Git, but they are built around different workloads. Vercel centers on frontends and serverless functions with a global CDN, Railway runs long-lived services and databases billed per second for the resources they use, and Render offers web services, workers, cron jobs and managed Postgres on fixed-size instances. As of Nov 2026, each has a $0 entry point: Vercel Hobby (personal, non-commercial use only), Railway's Free plan with $1 of monthly usage, and Render's Hobby workspace with free compute instances. Paid plans start at $5/month on Railway (Hobby), $20/month on Vercel Pro and $25/month on Render Pro, before usage or compute. In our view, Vercel fits frontend-heavy web apps, Railway fits small backends and side projects that need a database next to the app, and Render fits teams that want predictable per-instance prices. Which should you pick?

  • Pick Vercel if your app is mostly frontend plus serverless functions and you want preview deployments on every push.
  • Pick Railway if you want an app server and its database in one project and prefer paying only for the resources you actually use.
  • Pick Render if you want fixed monthly prices per service and a managed Postgres without usage-based surprises.
  • In our view, combining them is common: for example a Vercel frontend talking to an API and database hosted on Railway or Render.

FAQ

Which of the three is free?

As of Nov 2026, all three have a $0 option. Vercel Hobby is free for personal, non-commercial projects. Railway's Free plan includes $1 of usage a month, and new users get a one-time $5 trial credit. Render's Hobby workspace is $0 and offers free web service, Postgres and Key Value instances with usage limits. Check each vendor's site for current pricing.

Can I host a database on each of them?

Render offers managed Postgres and Redis-compatible Key Value. Railway runs databases as services billed by the same CPU, memory and volume rates as your app. Vercel doesn't host a database itself; its pricing page lists storage such as Blob, and databases are added through Marketplace integrations.

Can I use Vercel Hobby for a commercial site?

No. Vercel's pricing FAQ says Hobby is for personal, non-commercial use, and Pro is meant for professional and business use.

How does Railway billing differ from Render?

Railway meters CPU, memory and disk per second and subtracts your plan's included usage. Render charges a fixed monthly price per instance size, prorated by the second, on top of the workspace plan fee.
